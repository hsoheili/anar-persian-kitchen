How we cook

One charcoal grill, lit at three in the afternoon, seven days a week. Lamb and beef are trimmed and skewered by hand in the morning. Chicken sits overnight in saffron, onion and lemon. Nothing goes on the grill that was frozen.

Bread, yogurt and herbs arrive or are made fresh daily. The rice is steamed in the afternoon so the tahdig comes out of the pot near dinner service — when it's gone for the evening, it's gone. Stews are started in the morning and cook slowly until they're ready; we don't rush them and we don't reheat yesterday's.

Ask us about substitutions. Rice for salad, grilled vegetables instead of meat, sauce on the side, less salt, no butter on the rice — none of it is a problem, and none of it costs extra unless the swap involves a more expensive protein. Just tell your server when you order.

Dietary notes and allergens

We cook in one kitchen with one grill, so we'll always be straight with you about what we can and can't guarantee.

Vegetarian and vegan

Salad Shirazi, adasi, sekanjabin, faloodeh and sholeh zard are vegan as written. The vegetable kabab, adas polo and the mushroom ghormeh sabzi are vegan when we hold the butter and yogurt — tell your server and we'll plate them that way. Our saffron rice is finished with butter by default; we steam a portion without it every service for vegan guests, and if we've run out we'll say so rather than guess.

Gluten

Rice plates, kababs, most stews, Shirazi salad and faloodeh contain no gluten ingredients. Barbari bread, ash-e reshteh, haleem and baghlava do. We fry in dedicated oil, but bread is torn and handled throughout the kitchen, so we can't promise a coeliac-safe environment. If gluten is a medical issue, please tell us when you book and we'll talk it through honestly.

Nuts

Walnuts, pistachios and almonds are used daily and openly across the menu — in fesenjan, mast-o khiar, sabzi khordan, kaleh joosh, bastani and every dessert but faloodeh. Ground walnut is in the air of our prep area. If someone at your table has a severe nut allergy, call us before you come so we can plan the evening properly, including where we seat you.

Dairy, eggs and halal

Yogurt, feta and whey appear in many starters. Eggs are in the brunch dishes and mirza ghasemi. All our beef, lamb and chicken are halal, sourced from a supplier in Burnaby we've used since 2011. No pork is cooked in this kitchen and we don't cook with alcohol.

Telling us about an allergy

The best time is when you book — there's a notes field on the reservation form, and it comes straight to the pass. Second best is when you sit down, before you order. Either way, tell us the allergy and how serious it is, and we'll tell you exactly what we can make. We'd rather steer you to a different dish than hand you a plate we're unsure about.
